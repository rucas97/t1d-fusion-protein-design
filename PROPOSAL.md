# Research Proposal: Tri-Functional T1D Fusion Protein (v2.0)

## 1. Background & Gap
Type 1 Diabetes requires both immune tolerance and beta-cell regeneration. Existing single-agent therapies fail because they address only one arm of the disease. No reported fusion protein combines GLP-1R agonism, Treg recruitment, and ER-stress relief in a single molecule. Furthermore, generic linkers do not account for islet-specific biology.

## 2. Hypothesis
A single fusion protein (Human GLP-1 – PC2 Linker – MANF – Fcµ_R587A) will simultaneously target beta cells, suppress autoimmunity, and promote survival—with spatial activation in the islet microenvironment.

## 3. Outcomes (Completed)
- **Aim 1:** Designed and validated a beta-cell-optimized protease-responsive linker (Furin resistant, PC2 susceptible, highly solvent-exposed).
- **Aim 2:** Constructed and computationally validated the full tri-functional fusion protein (672 aa, high structural confidence).
- **Aim 3:** Predicted immunogenicity, stability, and developability; proposed an experimental validation plan (`WET_LAB_ROADMAP.md`).

## 4. Conclusion
While the computational pipeline confirms structural feasibility, it also revealed a critical vulnerability: the `GGGGS` linkers are predicted B-cell epitopes. Future iterations (v3.0) must address this ADA risk.

# Computational Design of a Beta-Cell-Targeted Tri-Functional Fusion Protein with a Protease-Responsive Linker for Type 1 Diabetes Therapy

## 1. Background & Gap
Type 1 Diabetes (T1D) requires both immune tolerance and beta-cell regeneration.
Existing single-agent therapies fail because they address only one arm of the disease.
No reported fusion protein combines GLP-1R agonism, Treg recruitment, and ER-stress
relief in a single molecule. Furthermore, generic linkers do not account for
islet-specific biology.

## 2. Hypothesis
A single fusion protein (Exendin-4 – linker – MANF – linker – Fcµ) with a
PC2-cleavable linker will simultaneously target beta cells, suppress autoimmunity,
and promote survival—with spatial activation in the islet microenvironment.

## 3. Specific Aims
- **Aim 1:** Design and in silico validate a beta-cell-optimized protease-responsive linker.
- **Aim 2:** Construct and computationally validate the full tri-functional fusion protein.
- **Aim 3:** Predict immunogenicity, stability, and developability; propose an
  experimental validation plan.

## 4. Methods
- Sequence retrieval (UniProt)
- Linker design (rational + protease site analysis via PROSPER)
- Fusion construction (in silico cloning)
- Structure prediction (Foldify / MultiFOLD)
- Docking (HDOCK / ClusPro)
- Immunogenicity (TITANiAN / ImmunoCheck)
All tools are free and web-based.

## 5. Expected Outcomes & Limitations
A computationally validated design ready for wet-lab collaboration.
Limitations: no experimental validation; relies on prediction accuracy.
